A reproducible spatial analysis of 998 major earthquake events (1995–2023), built with GeoPandas, Shapely and Plotly, and published as a static dashboard.

Spatial concentration

Concentration is defined as the share of cleaned events falling within the single most-occupied 10° latitude × 10° longitude cell. The most active cell spans 20°S–10°S latitude, 160°E–170°E longitude (near the Solomon Islands–Vanuatu arc), containing 80 of 998 events (8.0%). The five most active cells together account for 26.9% of all events.

Scale sensitivity

Concentration figures rise as grid cells get larger, since a bigger cell simply catches more of the nearby seismic belt (the modifiable areal unit problem). A 10° cell balances two failure modes: over-fragmenting the Pacific "Ring of Fire" (5°) versus collapsing multiple distinct fault systems into one cell (20°). The pattern, not the exact percentage, is the reliable takeaway.

Methods, interpretation & limitations

M

Methods

  • Source spreadsheet read in Colab, columns standardised, lat/lon/magnitude coerced to numeric.
  • Invalid and duplicate rows removed (2 of 1000 original rows).
  • Converted to a GeoPandas GeoDataFrame with Shapely Point geometries, EPSG:4326.
  • Validated geometry type, validity, and world-extent membership.
  • Concentration measured with a reproducible 10° lat/lon grid.
  • Globe and charts built with Plotly, exported as static HTML.
I

Interpretation

  • Strongest concentration follows the western Pacific "Ring of Fire" — Solomon Islands, Vanuatu, Indonesia, Papua New Guinea, Japan.
  • Pattern is consistent with active subduction-zone plate boundaries in that region.
  • Since every event is already M6.5+, the magnitude distribution reflects which earthquakes get global cataloguing attention, not seismicity as a whole.
L

Limitations

  • Dataset contains only major earthquakes (M6.5+), 1995–2023 — not a complete seismicity record.
  • Concentration map reflects major-earthquake reporting patterns, not overall tectonic hazard or risk.
  • Annual counts may reflect changing detection/cataloguing standards, not only true frequency change.
  • ~1/3 of records lack continent/country data, so the magnitude-by-continent chart uses a smaller subset.
  • This is a descriptive spatial index, not a seismic-hazard or risk assessment.

Dataset: Earthquake Dataset (1995–2023), sourced from Kaggle — kaggle.com/datasets/farazrahman/earthquake
